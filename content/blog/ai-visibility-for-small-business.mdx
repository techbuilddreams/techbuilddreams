## What AI visibility actually means

AI visibility is the odds that a generative AI tool — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews, Apple Intelligence, voice assistants — names your business when a buyer asks a relevant question. It's also called *answer engine optimization* (AEO) or *generative engine optimization* (GEO), and three things drive it:

1. **What the model knows from training.** Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google ingested a snapshot of the web. If your site was crawlable on that snapshot, you might be in there.
2. **What the model retrieves live.** Most consumer AI tools now run a web search before they answer and cite 3–10 sources in real time.
3. **How clearly your site explains who you are.** Schema.org structured data, clean copy, and a consistent name/address/phone make it trivial for an LLM to extract "what you do, who you help, where you're based" without guessing.

Training-data presence is mostly out of your control. The other two are not.

## The surfaces you're competing on

| Surface | How it decides | What helps you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini chat | Live web search + training | Clear H1/H2 that match the question wording |
| Perplexity | Live retrieval with citations | Concise paragraphs an extractor can quote |
| Google AI Overviews | Retrieval over top-ranking pages | Existing SEO rank + direct-answer content |
| Apple Intelligence, Siri | Apple Business Connect + web | Verified profile, hours, services |
| Voice assistants | Local + speakable schema | `speakable` schema, FAQ pages |

Notice the pattern: every surface rewards the same handful of things. You don't optimize for ChatGPT separately from optimizing for AI Overviews — they pull from a similar pool of signals.

## The seven-item checklist (one afternoon of work)

1. **Add Organization and LocalBusiness schema** to your site — `name`, `url`, `telephone`, `address`, `openingHoursSpecification`, and `knowsAbout`. This is what LLMs read first when grounding an answer about you.
2. **Write a real FAQ page** that answers the literal questions buyers ask out loud — "how much does X cost," "who do you work with," "where are you based." Wrap it in `FAQPage` schema with `speakable` selectors.
3. **Make your homepage's first paragraph a direct-answer sentence.** "Tech Build Dreams builds AI voice agents and mobile apps for small businesses in the Boston area" beats "We turn ideas into reality."
4. **Publish a portfolio with `ItemList` schema.** Generative tools love structured lists they can cite.
5. **Claim Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, and Bing Places.** Same name, address, phone, hours, and description across all three.
6. **Earn citations from directories and one or two real publications.** Generative tools weight third-party mentions heavily — one solid press hit beats ten directory links.
7. **Re-check every six months.** Models retrain. Surfaces change. Treat AI visibility like a Lighthouse score, not a one-time project.

## What about "AI visibility score" tools?

Tools that grade your site 0-100 for AI visibility are useful as a directional audit — they catch missing schema, vague copy, and broken structured data. They are not gospel. The score is a function of whatever rubric the vendor invented, and the underlying fixes are the same Schema.org and SEO work that has existed for a decade.

If a vendor's pitch is "you scored 83, pay us to fix it," ask them what specifically the 17-point gap is. If the answer is "we'll send you a report," that's a sales motion, not a product.
